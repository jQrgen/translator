You have: Alice → deposits 100 XEL → smart contract → performs an operation → Alice withdraws 110 XEL. In the classic model the contract has to operate to some extent on public values. After confidential operations are introduced, the contract can potentially work on: encrypted amount instead of: public amount. So the contract doesn't have to know: > "Alice deposited exactly 100 XEL". It can only verify cryptographically that: > "Alice deposited a correct amount and the operation satisfies the contract's conditions". That's a fundamental difference. --- And Pedersen commitments are very important here A Pedersen commitment lets you commit to a value without revealing it. Put simply: > "I have a value X, but I won't show you X. I'll show you a proof that everything checks out." Xelis already uses Pedersen commitments in its Twisted ElGamal construction.
